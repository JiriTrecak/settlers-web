    Golden standard: composition and material checkpoints
    1. Forked water surrounds a raised central foreground island; left channel travels from upper middle to lower left, right channel curves from upper right to lower middle. A connecting channel passes across the center.
    2. Large olive broadleaf tree crowns the upper central island. Its trunk splits above the ground and thick leafy lobes extend outside the top edge.
    3. Green drooping willow stands on the left bank at roughly 31% width / 36% height. A layered rose-gray rock shelf extends behind it.
    4. Large pink willow occupies the right foreground, with a dark brown branching trunk and long separate leaf strands. Its canopy spans roughly 70–87% width / 38–76% height.
    5. Gold birches accent the central island foreground and far right. Rust bushes and trees are sparse. The ground remains muted sage and sand.
    6. Central island rises above the water: sloping pale sandy edges, a narrow winding path, green shoulders, a large faceted boulder below the gold birch, dense white flower clusters on its left.
    7. Background top-left is a tall continuous angular cliff face, not a cluster of round boulders. Left and foreground bank rocks have large broad layered facets.
    8. Shoreline is uneven and soft. Slate-blue centers transition to pale shallows. Water carries large smooth ripples and subtle flow streaks, with intermittent foam, not a white outline.
    9. Reed colonies are taller, darker and more vertical than meadow grass. Lily pads form small groups in upper channels.
    10. Grass forms low spreading fans with bent broad blades, in pale straw, ochre and sage. It clusters along transitions, leaving open sand and visible ground within colonies.
    11. Flowers are groups of small white heads with occasional purple stems. Pebbles and fallen leaves are sparse accents. Avoid uniform random distribution.
    12. Warm illumination, pink beige sand, rose-gray rock, lavender-brown shade and readable green canopies are matched together. Material color alone cannot reproduce the final pixel color.
