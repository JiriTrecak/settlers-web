# The Queen's Guard — polished comic storyboard

Status: reviewed and polished treatment for the next drawing pass. Twelve comic pages summarize the twelve-mission Ant campaign. Each page has five or six sequential panels, 64 panels total. Specific staging choices below are the working version to draw; they remain open to later campaign revision. This is an illustrated campaign treatment, not an exhaustive mission walkthrough.

The existing images are an exploratory visual reference. This storyboard replaces their scene breakdown for the next generation pass. Campaign cornerstones are recorded in [World and campaign design](../../../docs/wiki/factions/story.md).

## Comic language and continuity

- Most panels show a readable action, expression, interaction or discovery at close or medium distance. Use wide views selectively to introduce new spaces, make battlefield geography understandable, or expose a major consequence.
- Insect scale persists in the details: a dew drop, a leaf seam, bark beneath a hand, an acorn helmet on a table. Monumental scenery does not need to occupy every panel.
- Each page starts from a situation and ends on a changed situation, decision or belief. Battles show a tactical problem and its resolution rather than another anonymous army panorama.
- Heroes remain visually distinct: General, veteran sword commander with dark-green cloak; Warden, old comrade with broad shield and olive mantle; Captain, younger former pupil with bright-green scarf and spear. These are working roles, not finalized names or designs.
- The Queen remains within her hive while alive. Her death happens off-screen. Neither the killing nor a definitive culprit appears in this storyboard.
- Recurring gesture: the Queen lays two fingers on the General's shoulder. Recognition at resurrection repeats it; the command that follows turns the emotional memory into horror. A private phrase on page 2 returns through the unidentified voice on page 11.
- A recurring shield-bearer links the ordinary army to the heroes: an orange-red Ant with a chipped acorn helmet and an ivory leaf-shaped repair on their wood shield. Introduce them on page 1, save them with Root on page 4, and let their refusal to murder a captive expose the takeover on page 12.
- Working Root property: it renews and reinforces living tissue. The Captain's early process saves a wounded but clearly living soldier and enables stronger troops. A later siege ram uses a living-root frame whose fibers need continued Root supply to withstand the load. Neither application proves resurrection. Exact economy and unit mechanics are outside this treatment.
- The Queen's remains travel in a resin-lined leaf cocoon on a bark bier, accompanied by the same two bearers. Show this on pages 8–12. This is a visual continuity choice, not an additional quest or resource mechanic.
- The Warden returns home on page 9 because a new attack changes the expedition's agreed terms. The General and Captain begin escaping together on page 12; their eventual fates are not resolved here. The later coalition finale is outside these twelve pages.
- Dialogue below is provisional and brief. Silent reactions should carry much of the story. Page titles are working titles.

## Page 01 — The Queen's Banner

Mission focus: establish a base and break the siege of a frontier position. Story change: the General rescues the Captain and demonstrates why soldiers trust them.

1. **The failing defense — medium shot.** The Captain braces a splintered gate beside two exhausted soldiers. One has the chipped helmet and repaired shield that will identify our recurring shield-bearer. An impact shakes loose bark overhead.
2. **Recognition — close-up.** The shield-bearer notices the General's banner through the gate gap. Their clenched jaw relaxes. Optional line: “The General.”
3. **A commander among soldiers — medium shot.** The General's small advance party has reached the trapped defenders through a narrow service tunnel; the tunnel mouth and arriving veteran make the route visible. Behind cover, the General steadies the shield-bearer's shaking hands: “Stay with me.” Incoming missiles keep the moment urgent. The main relief force is still outside, building its foothold for the counterattack.
4. **The tactical breakthrough — action shot.** The Captain opens the side gate as the General's relief force attacks the siege line's exposed flank. A broken barricade and overturned enemy siege device make the change in the battle legible.
5. **Teacher and pupil — two-shot.** The General clasps the Captain's forearm, then notices their injured arm. Relief gives way to affectionate reproach. General: “You held too long.” Captain: “You came.”

## Page 02 — Beyond the Boundary

Mission focus: capture a passage that enables expansion. Story change: the Queen's ambition becomes a shared undertaking; the Warden enters the company.

1. **The Queen's trust — intimate two-shot.** Inside the hive, the General privately reports the soldiers recovered from the siege before speaking of victory. The Queen places two fingers on their shoulder: “You count the missing first. That is why I chose you.” Her affection rests on a quality the reader has already seen.
2. **Old comrades — overhead medium shot.** The Warden weighs down a curled leaf map with their shield boss, revealing an alternate approach. The Captain reaches to mark a bold direct assault; the General waits for the Warden's explanation. Familiarity makes their disagreement comfortable.
3. **The price of control — small-group shot.** At the passage, a rival Ant officer blocks a colony caravan and presents terms. The General sees their own people waiting behind the confrontation. Political dominance has a concrete military expression.
4. **Taking command — action shot.** The Warden challenges the defenders outside their strongest position, drawing them into a shield-line clash. The General captures their exposed command perch while the Captain cuts off reinforcements. The victory changes control of the passage rather than repeating the previous siege relief.
5. **The next ambition — medium shot.** Back in the royal chamber, the Queen moves a marker beyond the conquered passage: “No colony should decide how far ours can grow.” The General accepts the commission; the Warden taps the captured route with practical satisfaction. The Captain sees the scale of what they are joining.

## Page 03 — Beneath the Fallen Giant

Mission focus: hero exploration beneath a fallen tree. Story change: Root enters as a discovery, with an ambiguous first sign of something responsive underground.

1. **A different kind of expedition — wide establishing shot.** The three heroes descend through a crack in a fallen trunk. Their lights are small against bark layers and hanging fungal threads. This establishes the space once.
2. **Danger at their own scale — close action shot.** A centipede leg punches through a rotten ledge beside the Captain. The Warden catches their scarf and pulls them back; the General holds the creature off. Only part of its body fits in the frame.
3. **First contact — detail shot.** The Captain clears soil with the butt of their spear, exposing a living Root filament beneath an old carving. Its glow reflects in their eyes. It remains part of the tree, not a loose fantasy crystal.
4. **An observable property — close-up.** A crushed moss stem beside the exposed filament straightens and greens; the Captain compares it with an untreated stem. An adjacent carving repeats the same branching pattern. Restoration and the ancient site are connected visually without proving resurrection.
5. **An unanswered response — medium shot.** A pulse runs down the roots and a buried shutter withdraws, exposing the route back out. The Captain reaches it before the mechanism moves again. The General looks toward the source of this apparently helpful intervention. No face, villain silhouette or explanation appears.

## Page 04 — A New Strength

Mission focus: secure and process Root to overcome an enemy counterattack. Story change: Root saves a familiar life as well as winning a battle; all three heroes have reason to trust it.

1. **The old limit — medium action shot.** An enemy assault device breaks the ordinary shield line. The familiar shield-bearer falls, badly injured. The Warden drags them clear while the Captain sees the shattered defenses.
2. **A life still present — close-up.** At the field processing station, a healer confirms the soldier is breathing but failing. With the General's authorization, the Captain applies their experimental Root preparation. The living tissue begins to recover. Keep the breath and healer visible so this cannot read as resurrection.
3. **Trust earned — two-shot.** The soldier grips the Warden's wrist and sits up. Warden, to the Captain: “You saved them.” The Captain's relief matches the Warden's; both believed the experiment could fail.
4. **A military breakthrough — action shot.** Troops strengthened through the same process hold the assault device back long enough to bring it down. The Captain directs them; the General commits the counterattack. Root supports the army rather than making one hero's weapon inexplicably magical.
5. **Recognition from the Queen — intimate shot.** In the hive, the Queen fastens an insignia to the Captain's scarf: “Your commander told me who kept our soldiers alive.” The Captain looks toward the General, who gives a small proud nod.
6. **Shared confidence — medium shot.** The Queen seals orders for more extraction. The General and Warden accept their assignments; the Captain is included at the planning table for the first time. This is a success they all believe in.

## Page 05 — The Crown's Reach

Mission focus: a major offensive supported by sustained Root access. Story change: the Ants reach the height of their success just as home falls silent.

1. **A stronger army — medium shot.** The Captain orders a siege ram forward. Its living-root frame strains under the load; workers replenish its Root-fed reservoir so its fibers remain strong while ordinary troops protect them. This shows why the highest-tier force needs sustained access rather than another war-council map.
2. **The soldier we saved — close-up.** The recovered shield-bearer takes their place beside the General and adjusts the repaired shield. General: “Still with me?” The soldier nods. Their returned confidence embodies the real benefit of Root.
3. **The decisive action — medium action shot.** The ram breaks the reinforced enemy position that ordinary troops could not overcome. The enemy commander lowers their weapon as the General's army advances. The workers maintaining the ram's Root supply remain visible in the background.
4. **A rare easy moment — close group shot.** The Captain tries to imitate the General's formal victory address, earning a tired laugh from both veterans. Their empty place for the Queen's reply is still ordinary expectation.
5. **Silence from home — close-up.** A messenger hands the General a dispatch whose reply section remains blank. Messenger: “No one answers.” The General's smile disappears. No cutaway shows what happened at the hive.

## Page 06 — The Silent Hive

Mission focus: a hero-led penetration through the fractured colony to reach and recover the Queen. Story change: her death becomes certain while its cause remains unknown. The larger battle to regain command belongs to the following mission.

1. **Home has changed — wide establishing shot.** The Guard stops above the familiar hive entrance. Smoke rises from one district; rival banners divide another. A once-busy route lies deserted. Geography shows turmoil without complete destruction.
2. **Contradictory orders — medium shot.** A familiar sentry is relieved to recognize the General but shows them an order sealing the royal district: “They said you weren't coming back.” The order is unsigned or issued under emergency authority, providing uncertainty rather than identifying a murderer.
3. **A way through — action shot.** The small Guard company crosses between rival positions while the Warden shields civilians leaving the royal tunnel. The Captain secures a narrow entrance. The absent main army keeps this visually distinct from retaking the colony.
4. **The last door — intimate shot.** A surviving royal attendant meets the heroes at the chamber and cannot speak. The General opens the door personally; the Captain and Warden stop behind them. No extra hand-blocking gesture delays the reveal.
5. **The loss — still medium shot.** The Queen lies motionless on her familiar resting platform, without visible wounds or incriminating objects. The General kneels beside her; the Captain remains at the threshold; the Warden's shield slips toward the floor. An attendant confirms that the colony's healers found her dead. Leave this panel visually quiet.
6. **Consequence without explanation — close-up.** The General takes the Queen's hand; it offers no answering gesture. Through the doorway, rival orders and fighting continue. The Warden turns toward that sound while the Captain watches the General.

## Page 07 — No One Commands

Mission focus: defeat a rival command position and restore enough order for the colony to survive. Story change: the Guard regains military control but disagrees about what comes next.

1. **The claim — medium shot.** A rival Ant commander occupies the colony's command hall and demands allegiance. The General refuses while the Captain notices familiar soldiers among the opposing ranks. No visual implies this commander killed the Queen.
2. **A choice in battle — action close-up.** The Warden knocks a rival soldier's blade aside and orders their own troops to accept surrender. The General advances toward the commander. Civil conflict involves people they know.
3. **A victory with an unanswered question — medium shot.** The rival standard comes down. A loyal officer asks the General who now leads. The General offers immediate defensive orders but cannot name a future for the throne.
4. **A fragile settlement — group shot.** The General entrusts a loyal commander with the repaired defenses. The Warden makes the limit explicit: “The main army stays. Whatever we do next.” The General agrees. The home colony is provisionally secure before anyone leaves it.
5. **Hope enters the argument — two-shot.** In a secured chamber beside the reclaimed command hall, the same royal attendants prepare the Queen's covered remains for their funerary cocoon. The Captain holds the old Root sample beside the General: “It saved the shield-bearer. Those ruins may tell us what else it can do.” The General looks up. Their shared experience makes the question credible without promising resurrection.

## Page 08 — What the Root Remembers

Mission focus: return to the buried ruins and discover a possible restoration site. Story change: a fragile theory becomes a destination the General chooses to pursue.

1. **Back into danger — medium action shot.** The heroes cut through changed roots blocking the old passage. The Warden protects the Captain as they recover markings first noticed on the earlier expedition.
2. **Evidence, not an answer — close-up.** Cleaning the carving reveals a covered crowned figure carried on a funerary bier into a recognizable tree opening, then standing with the same crown. The treatment is depicted as a rite for the dead, making the inference stronger than a generic fallen figure. The final section of the carving is missing.
3. **A real possibility — tight group shot.** The Captain compares the carving's Root pattern with the process that saved the soldier. The Warden examines the broken final section: “We can reach it. We still don't know how this ended.” The General studies the restored figure. The Warden supports investigation without claiming certainty.
4. **A destination and a limit — overhead detail.** A surviving route inscription identifies the sanctuary. The General marks it on their map; the Warden lays down a small expedition marker while the main-army marker remains at the hive. They agree to a limited expedition.
5. **A private loss — close-up.** Back at the hive, the General sits beside the covered Queen and rests a hand on their own shoulder where she touched it. No apparition answers. This quiet panel makes the expedition's motive personal.
6. **The departure — medium shot.** The Queen's resin-lined leaf cocoon on its bark bier passes through the hive gate with the two bearers and the limited escort. The General walks beside it, the Captain carries the map, and the Warden takes a place at the front. This is a shared undertaking, not an expedition opposed by one hero from its beginning.

## Page 09 — The Price of Passage

Mission focus: break the army blocking the route to the restoration site. Story change: the General and Warden separate over their shared duty to the colony.

1. **A route with a price — medium shot.** The expedition is pinned at a crossing while an enemy force attacks its rear. The General orders a forward base held and commits the escort's reserve so the Queen's bier can pass. The war is now protecting an increasingly personal undertaking.
2. **Victory won together — action shot.** The Warden holds the rear crossing while the Captain's troops destroy the enemy's attack position. The General escorts the bier and surviving forces forward. A recognizable crossing, rather than another opened gate, is the tactical focus.
3. **The terms change — intimate two-shot.** A messenger brings evidence of a new attack on the home colony. The Warden places the report before the General: “They need us now.” The General authorizes troops to return but intends to continue with the remainder.
4. **A painful admission — closer shot.** General: “I cannot leave her dead.” Warden: “Then let me keep someone alive for you to return to.” The Captain hears both. The Warden's choice expresses affection and duty, not a triumphant warning.
5. **The separation — medium composition.** The General clasps the Warden's forearm, echoing the earlier rescue. The Warden returns with the defensive contingent while the Captain and the familiar shield-bearer stay beside the bier. Their paths separate around an ordinary root.

## Page 10 — The Forbidden Approach

Mission focus: establish a foothold and defeat the sanctuary's outer defenses. Story change: the Guard encounters informed opposition and chooses to continue.

1. **The threshold — selective wide shot.** The funeral expedition reaches the hollow tree's lower entrance. Bee fortifications interrupt the route, with wax, leaf and root construction clearly different from Ant works. Bees hold the ground with folded wings.
2. **A person who understands — medium shot.** A Bee commander recognizes the crowned bier and orders their troops to hold fire. They speak directly to the General. Commander: “You cannot carry her through that gate.”
3. **A warning with an alternative — tight group shot.** The commander shows a damaged old record of a restoration that ended in loss of control and offers guarded passage home. Their order will not open the sanctuary because opening it breaches containment. The Captain can compare real evidence; the General sees a documented possibility of restoration and an uncertain account of its failure. General: “You know it can work.” Commander: “I know what followed.” The choice is a conscious risk, not a consequence of the Bees refusing to explain anything.
4. **The chosen assault — medium action shot.** The Guard attacks a specific outer fortification; the Captain disables its defense while the General takes the position. The Bee commander retreats toward the sealed inner gate.
5. **A warning kept — medium shot.** The captured Bee commander surrenders. The General orders them spared and secured with the other prisoners. The Captain keeps the record from their earlier exchange. They will use it to take a precaution rather than forget what they heard.

## Page 11 — A Voice Within

Mission focus: the General and Captain enter the inner site to open the army's final approach. Story change: they proceed on uncertain evidence, with an apparent safeguard, while the unidentified presence speaks directly to the General's grief.

1. **The last resistance — close action shot.** The two heroes defeat an ancient guardian beside the inner mechanism. The General catches the Captain before a collapsing ledge takes them. Their old teacher–pupil bond remains visible.
2. **Recognition through a voice — intimate shot.** From somewhere within the chamber, an indistinct voice repeats the private words from page 2: “You count the missing first.” The General turns as if the Queen were beside them. No apparition or visible villain confirms the speaker. The voice supplies apparent evidence of her presence, not a verified identity.
3. **A precaution — medium detail.** Using the Bee record, the Captain closes an exposed secondary channel at a valve beside a small service exit. That opening leads to a side root passage, separate from the army's main entrance; keep its distinct broken-leaf arch identifiable. The Captain orders the main force held beyond a visible outer boundary, believing they have limited the ritual's reach. No diagram tells them that the army will be possessed; the roots' buried connections are unknown.
4. **The choice acknowledged — intimate two-shot.** Captain: “A voice is not proof.” General: “Neither is that broken record. We try. If she is not herself, we stop.” The Captain looks toward the isolated platform and gives a reluctant nod. The agreement is explicit and limited, although neither understands how quickly control can be lost.
5. **An irreversible commitment — hand detail.** The General opens the inner gate. The two bearers advance with the cocoon; the Captain checks the closed channel once more. The decision and safeguard are both visible.

## Page 12 — The Queen Returns

Mission focus: defeat the inner defenders, secure the source and complete the resurrection. Story change: military victory and personal hope become immediate catastrophe.

1. **The hard-won objective — medium action shot.** The Guard defeats the last inner defenders and activates the secured source. The General places the Queen on the platform; the Captain monitors the isolated channel. The surviving army and Bee prisoners wait beyond the outer boundary near the main entrance. The Captain's separate side exit remains clear.
2. **Life — extreme close-up.** The Queen's fingers move against the General's hand. One eye opens. The frame contains almost nothing else. Give the apparent success its own moment.
3. **Recognition — intimate group shot.** The Queen lays two fingers on the General's shoulder, repeating page 2. The General's relief is unmistakable; the Captain laughs through tears. Beyond the boundary, the familiar shield-bearer lifts their repaired shield in celebration.
4. **The first command — medium shot.** The Queen points toward the surrendered Bee commander: “Kill them.” Ant soldiers move in unnatural unison. The familiar shield-bearer plants their shield between them and the prisoner: “The General gave their word.” The General turns, still struggling to understand what they heard.
5. **The betrayal — tight action shot.** Controlled soldiers strike down the shield-bearer. Their identifiable repaired shield falls beside the captive. The General's “Stand down!” has no effect. Beneath the supposedly safe boundary, Root filaments flare and spread toward the waiting ranks; the Captain sees why their precaution failed. Show the death clearly without gore.
6. **The catastrophe expands — final wide shot with readable foreground.** The Captain pulls the General toward the separate service exit with its broken-leaf arch while the General reaches back toward the Queen. The exposed channel remains closed, but buried roots emerge throughout the army's position and troops turn on comrades. In the distance, the possessed Queen rises within the enormous trunk. The pupil now saves the mentor; the loss spreads into the forest. The shot begins their escape without resolving later fates or revealing the true entity's full form.

## Drawing handoff

- Preserve the panel counts, chronological order, identifiable shield-bearer, shoulder gesture, Queen's cocoon and two bearers, and Captain's visible precaution. These carry the payoffs.
- Draw one moment per panel. Descriptions of a process specify the selected instant and any necessary adjacent evidence; do not compress several sequential actions into one image.
- Page 6 is the quietest page. Leave room around the death reveal and keep narration sparse. Page 12 uses two intimate relief panels before the first lethal command; do not replace them with scenery.
- Expressions and gestures must remain readable at normal page size. Restrict dialogue to the supplied short exchanges; captions need not restate visible actions.
- Wide shots are reserved principally for the first underground descent, return to the hive, sanctuary threshold, and final catastrophe. Other battle panels show named participants accomplishing a specific action.
- Ordinary forest details maintain scale in intimate scenes. Ant and Bee architecture remains built from bark, roots, leaves, acorns, resin and wax. Bees fight on the ground; no permanent aerial army.
- New drawings should adapt the original Ant character reference and this treatment. Existing pages are style experiments and must not override revised staging, panel count or chronology.

## Later campaign questions retained

The Queen's killer and exact death mechanism remain unrevealed. The ancient entity's identity, the full nature of Root, the Queen's inner experience, the Captain's eventual fate and the later coalition liberation remain open. None requires drawing a placeholder answer into this Ant-campaign ending.
